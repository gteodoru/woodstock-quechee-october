Four days in Woodstock & Quechee

A calm, kid-paced foliage trip for Gabriel, his wife, their 22-month-old, and the au pair. Woodstock as a soft-yes base. Mornings out, naps at lodging, early dinners, bedtime at 8.

  • Old Greenwich → US-4

The plan at a glance

Overview

Why Woodstock

A soft yes as the base: a real village green, covered bridge, farm, and national park within a short drive of Quechee. Enough for adults and an au pair without stacking tourist-trap days. Stay west of the village if you can — kitchen + living room beats a hotel room when a toddler needs a reliable nap.

The drive

Old Greenwich, CT to Woodstock is about 4 hours 15 minutes of pure driving, or closer to 5 hours with a toddler stop. Route: I-95 → I-91 → US-4. Leave with a full snack kit. Aim to arrive with enough afternoon left for a soft landing, not a big attraction.

Early October foliage

This is the southern Vermont peak window — more color than mid-September, and more crowds. Weekends and Route 4 overlooks get busy. Book lodging first, reserve Friday/Saturday dinner, and keep afternoons short so you are not hunting for parking at 3 p.m. with a waking toddler.

Daily rhythm

  1. Morning Out before ~12
  2. Nap 12:30–2:30 at lodging
  3. Afternoon Back by ~6–6:30
  4. Dinner ~5:30–6:30
  5. Bed 8:00 p.m.

Day 2 · Woodstock

Billings Farm, then the park and village

The one farm morning. Do not add VINS today — two ticketed outdoor sites will blow the nap and the mood.

Hours
Open daily 10 a.m.–5 p.m. from late May through October 31. Peak-season tickets are often good for two consecutive days — confirm when you buy.
Why it fits
A working Jersey dairy, barns, and a restored 1890 farmhouse. Animals + wide paths, not a theme park. Harvest weekend (early October) is festive; arrive at opening.
Toddler note
Children under 3 are free. Two hours is enough: animals, a barn wander, maybe the farmhouse if he is still happy. Skip stacking more after.
Stroller
All-terrain or sturdy stroller. Paths are farm-grade, not mall-smooth. Some barn thresholds are bumpy.

Billings Farm Hours & admission Advance tickets

Why it fits
Billings shares a parking lot with the national park — tempting to stay. Go home. The carriage roads are better after he sleeps.
Toddler note
If he crashes in the car, transfer quietly; do not “just stop in the village.”
Adults / au pair
Kitchen lunch, then a book or a walk on the inn grounds.
Hours
Carriage roads and trails: dawn to dusk. Carriage Barn Visitor Center: Thursday–Monday, 10 a.m.–5 p.m., through October 31 (closed Tue–Wed).
Why it fits
The same Billings parking lot. A short stretch of historic carriage road is foliage without a hike. Then the village green and Vermont’s oldest continuously operated bookstore.
Toddler note
Stay on the wide carriage roads. Skip steep connectors (Precipice, etc.). One loop, then books and a snack.
Stroller
All-terrain helps on packed gravel. There is a short steep walk from the farm lot up to the Carriage Barn — one adult can take the toddler while another brings the stroller, or stay on the lower paths.

Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller NHP Yankee Bookshop

If you did not do Worthy on Day 1 and it is Wed–Sun, this is a good second chance. Otherwise Farmers’ Market leftovers. Lights out at 8.

Day 3 · Quechee

VINS in the morning, something small after the nap

Raptors and the Beaver Lodge for under-3s. Skip the treehouse / canopy platforms that mean stairs with a toddler in arms.

Hours
VINS Nature Center, 149 Natures Way, Quechee — daily 10 a.m.–5 p.m. Live bird programs run on a posted daily schedule.
Why it fits
Eagles and owls at toddler eye level, plus an indoor Beaver Lodge designed for ages 3 and under. Adults get a serious nature center, not a gift-shop stop.
Toddler note
Do the raptor walk and Beaver Lodge. Skip the Forest Canopy Walk / treehouse platforms with stairs — carrying a 22-month-old on those decks is the wrong kind of adventure.
Stroller
Main campus walkways and the Beaver Lodge are accessible. Some trail spurs are not. Park the stroller at the Welcome Center if a path narrows.

VINS Visit & accessibility Exhibits, including Beaver Lodge

VINS is on the Quechee side. Budget drive time so you are not still in the parking lot at 12:40. Picnic after he wakes, not instead of the nap.

Access
Gorge: Route 4 bridge sidewalks only. Simon Pearce mill: 1760 Quechee Main Street; glassblowing can be viewed daily (hours vary by weekday — check the Quechee page before you go).
Why it fits
A short wow after the nap. Glass is warm and indoor if the weather turns. The overlook is five minutes if the light is good.
Toddler note
Glass studio is a look, not a linger — hot work, no climbing. At the gorge, stay on the bridge; skip the steep trail again.
Dinner note
Simon Pearce has a restaurant. Dinner there only if everyone is calm — it is a nicer room than a toddler witching hour usually wants.

Day 4 · Departure

Sugarbush Farm, then point the car south

A free, short farm morning. Be on the road around noon so the drive home can eat the nap.

Hours
591 Sugarbush Farm Road, Woodstock. Open 9 a.m.–5 p.m. weekdays, weekends, and holidays. Free samples and a self-guided maple walk. No reservation.
Why it fits
Cheese and maple without a ticket line. Goats in the warm months, a sugarhouse you can peek into, and a country store for gifts that are actually useful.
Toddler note
Ninety minutes is plenty. Gravel road in — leave extra time, and do not cut it close to a noon departure.
Stroller
Mixed: farmyard and store are fine; the maple walk is nicer worn or with a carrier if the ground is wet.

Sugarbush Farm Hours & directions

Why it fits

Toddler note
Same stop logic as the drive up. Do not add one more “quick” attraction after the farm.
Pack-out

Where you sleep

Lodging

Kitchen + living room is the whole strategy. Ground floor if you can get it. Confirm a crib before you put down a deposit.

Primary

On The River Inn · Farmhouse 2BR

1653 W Woodstock Rd, Woodstock

Separate farmhouse building: full kitchen, living/dining, two bedrooms, two baths. Prefer the ground-floor courtyard two-bedroom so you are not carrying a sleeping toddler up stairs. Hotel perks (indoor pool, s’mores) sit behind a nightly amenity fee — confirm the current amount when you book.

  • Ask for a crib / pack-and-play in writing
  • West of the village — quieter, easy nap return
  • 506 Bistro is on site if you need a backup meal

On The River Inn Ground-floor Farmhouse 2BR

Farm-stay alt

Fat Sheep · Four Corners Cabin

Hartland / Woodstock–Quechee area

A real working-farm cabin with kitchen and porch. Fine for four people — loft caveat: the second queen is up a spiral stair, which is a poor place for a 22-month-old. Use the loft for adults/au pair only, or skip this option if the layout feels wrong.

Where you eat

Dining

Hours below are only what is listed here or on the linked official pages. Call or check the site the week you go.

Worthy Kitchen

Farm-to-table, kids menu, Resy. Wed–Fri from 4; Sat & Sun earlier (from noon). Closed Mon–Tue. Book Fri/Sat. Walk-ins exist but foliage weekends fill.

worthyvermont.com Resy

Angkor Wat

Thai/Cambodian in the village. From 4. BYOB. A good Day 3 dinner if you want something quieter than Worthy. Confirm hours that week — seasonal places shift.

Woodstock Farmers’ Market

The kitchen-night backbone: groceries, prepared food, coffee. Flagship on W. Woodstock Road, handy to On The River. Closed Mondays.

woodstockfarmersmarket.com

Mon Vert Café

Casual breakfast/lunch on Central Street. First come, first served — useful after the bookshop or before a morning activity. Check their site for the closed day.

monvertcafe.com

Mountain Creamery

Casual breakfast, lunch, and ice cream west of the village. A low-stakes meal if the toddler needs pancakes, not a reservation.

Simon Pearce Restaurant

Beautiful room over the falls. Dinner only if the table is calm — treat it as optional, not a plan you force on Day 3. Peek at glassblowing instead.
